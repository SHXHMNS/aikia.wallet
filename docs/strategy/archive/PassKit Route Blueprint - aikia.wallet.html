04

Setup steps

STEP 1 · DAY 1

Make the demo card

  1. Start the PassKit free trial with a company email, not a personal one.
  2. Create a new Membership / Loyalty project and name it "aikia demo".
  3. Design the card: logo, the Ink colour, strip image, and fields for Member, Stamps, Tier and The Usual.
  4. Create three tiers named Ink, Chrome and Pink, each with its own colours.
  5. Add one location (latitude and longitude) with a lock-screen message for the Halo nudge.
  6. Open the sign-up link, add the card to an iPhone and an Android phone, and scan it with PassKit's PassReader app.
Done when: the aikia-styled card is on both phones and a scan changes the stamp count. This is enough to show a café owner.
STEP 2 · DAYS 2–3

Ask PassKit five questions

  1. Do your terms allow us to resell this to venues under our own brand?
  2. Apple cards use our own certificate. Can Google cards also sit under our own Google issuer account?
  3. What do the custom domain and branding add-ons cost?
  4. Is there one project per venue, or can one project hold many venue designs? How is each counted for billing?
  5. Where is member data stored, and can we export all of it at any time?
Done when: the answers are in writing. Question 2 decides how painful it is to leave later.
STEP 3 · DAYS 3–5

Connect by API

  1. In the PassKit portal, create API credentials. Store them only as server secrets.
  2. Create our Next.js project on Vercel and a Supabase database, as in the Developer Blueprint steps 0 and 1.
  3. Write one small file, lib/passkit.ts, with five functions: enrol a member, read a member, update a member's fields, change a member's tier, and update many members at once.
  4. Subscribe to PassKit webhooks so our server is told when a card is installed, removed or updated.
  5. Test each function against the demo project.
Done when: a script on our server can create a member and change their stamps, and both phones update.
STEP 4 · WEEK 2

Build the join page and web scanner

  1. Join page at /join/[venue]: name, phone, consent. It saves the member in our database, enrols them in PassKit and shows the Add to Wallet button.
  2. Web scanner at /scan: staff log in with a PIN, the camera reads the card's QR, and buttons add a stamp or redeem a reward.
  3. Make the scanner installable: add a web app manifest and a service worker so staff can save it to the home screen like an app. It must run on HTTPS for the camera to work.
  4. Every scan writes a row to our own ledger first, then tells PassKit to update the card.
Done when: a full visit works without opening any PassKit screen.
STEP 5 · WEEKS 2–3

Dashboard, automation and venue admin

  1. Owner dashboard: members, visits, top guests, lapsed guests, all read from our database.
  2. Fill My Tables: a button that updates an offer field on every member of that venue, then clears it after 45 minutes.
  3. Daily job: birthdays, win-back offers and tier upgrades.
  4. Venue admin: our internal form that stores a venue's details and its PassKit project and tier IDs.
Done when: a second fake venue runs side by side with the first and nothing crosses over.
STEP 6 · WEEK 3

Pilot

  1. Move the PassKit project from test to live so its branding leaves the card.
  2. Run one friendly café for two weeks.
  3. Work through the launch gates in the Launch Readiness Blueprint. They apply to this route too.
Done when: two weeks pass with no wrong count and no data mix-up.
05

What we build ourselves

Guest-facing

Join page

Styled per venue from our database. Collects consent. Calls PassKit to enrol, then hands the guest the card link.

Staff-facing

Web scanner (PWA)

A web page that installs to the home screen. Camera scan, PIN login, cooldown and daily cap checks, then the update call.

Owner-facing

Dashboard

Numbers and the offer button. Reads our database, so it stays fast and carries only our brand.

Internal

Venue admin

Add a venue, store its PassKit IDs, print its QR. Card design itself is done in the PassKit portal.

Background

Automation jobs

Scheduled tasks for birthdays, win-back, offer expiry and tier moves. Each one ends in a PassKit update call.

Safety

Our own ledger

Every stamp, redemption and balance change is recorded in our database. PassKit shows the result. We hold the truth.

Not built in this route: pass signing, Apple's web service routes, push to Apple, Google class and object code. That is the work PassKit replaces.

07

Managing it day to day

Per sale

New venue runbook

  1. Collect the venue sheet: logo, colours, strip photo, map pin, reward rule, Halo message.
  2. In PassKit, duplicate the template project and apply the venue's design to all three tiers.
  3. Add the location and lock-screen message.
  4. In our admin, create the venue and paste in the PassKit IDs.
  5. Add the card to both test phones and do three practice scans.
  6. Print the QR stand, train staff, send the owner their dashboard login.

Target: one to two hours per venue.

Every week

Routine

  • Compare card counts in PassKit with members in our database. They should match.
  • Check for failed update calls and retry them.
  • Review stamps per staff member for anything unusual.
  • Look at card removals after each campaign.
Every month

Money check

  • Read the PassKit invoice and divide it by venue.
  • Flag any venue whose card volume makes it unprofitable on its plan.
  • Remove cards of members who deleted them long ago, if PassKit still bills for them.
Access

Who can touch what

  • PassKit login: one or two aikia people only, with two-step login. Each seat adds to the bill.
  • API credentials: on the server only.
  • Venues and staff: our pages only, never PassKit.

Limits and exit plan

RiskWhat it meansWhat we do
Dependence on one supplierIf PassKit raises prices, changes rules or has an outage, all venues are affected.Keep our own ledger and member list complete at all times, so the cards can be rebuilt elsewhere.
Leaving laterApple cards are signed with our own certificate, which helps. Google cards and the address cards update from may still tie us to PassKit.Ask for our own Google issuer too, and test in the trial how cards would be moved.
Dollar billingOur cost moves with the exchange rate and grows with every member.Track cost per venue monthly. Plan our own engine once the PassKit bill passes the cost of maintaining it.
Data held abroadMember names and numbers sit on PassKit's servers as well as ours.Name PassKit in our privacy policy as a processor. Send it only the fields the card needs.
Their limits become oursDesign options, message rules and update speed are whatever PassKit allows.Test each headline feature in the trial before promising it to a venue.
When to switch to our own engine

Stay on PassKit until about 25 paying venues or a monthly PassKit bill that would fund a developer's time. The Developer Blueprint is the plan for that switch, and our ledger makes it possible.
