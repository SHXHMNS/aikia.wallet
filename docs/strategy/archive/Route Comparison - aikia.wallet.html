Which route do we take?

PassKit or our own engine.

A side-by-side comparison of renting PassKit against building the pass engine ourselves, with a clear recommendation for a founder who is not a developer.

Recommendation

Start on PassKit. Build your own engine later, if the business earns it.

You are selling a service to cafés, not software to engineers. PassKit removes the hardest and riskiest technical work, lets you show a real card this week, and costs little until venues are paying. Your own engine only becomes worth it at around 25 venues or more.

01

Do you still pay Apple on the PassKit route?

Short answer

Yes. You pay Apple US$99 a year on both routes.

PassKit states that live Apple Wallet cards must be signed with a certificate from your own Apple Developer account, and that there is no alternative. So the PassKit route costs the Apple fee plus PassKit's fees. Google's wallet account is free on both routes.

There is an upside. Because the cards carry your certificate and not PassKit's, they belong to your Apple account. That makes a later move to your own engine far easier.

05

Why I recommend PassKit first

Reason 1

The real risk is sales, not software

Nobody knows yet whether cafés will pay ₹9,999 a month. Find that out in weeks with a rented engine before spending a month building one.

Reason 2

The hardest part is the part you rent

Signing cards, certificates and pushing updates to two wallet systems is where a non-developer gets stuck. Scanner and dashboard pages are far easier to build with AI help.

Reason 3

Mistakes cost less

The two promises, correct counts and private lists, are easier to keep when a specialist runs the engine and you focus on one small layer.

Reason 4

Nothing is wasted

The join page, scanner, dashboard, ledger and venue process you build on PassKit all carry over when you switch engines.

What would change my answer

Choose your own engine from day one only if

  • you have a committed developer or technical co-founder, and
  • you can wait a month or more before the first sale, and
  • owning the technology matters more to you right now than proving demand.

This is a business judgement based on what you have told me. The numbers behind it are estimates, and the decision is yours.

The path to follow

STAGE 1 · WEEKS 1–3

Sell with no code

  • Join the Apple Developer Program
  • Start the PassKit trial and design the demo card
  • Use PassKit's scanner app for the first pilot café
  • Run 20 venue meetings

Goal: one to three cafés say yes.

STAGE 2 · MONTHS 1–3

Add the aikia layer

  • Build our own join page, web scanner and dashboard
  • Keep every stamp in our own ledger
  • Add Fill My Tables and tiers
  • Pass the launch gates

Goal: 10 paying venues, nothing shows PassKit.

STAGE 3 · ABOUT 25 VENUES

Decide on our own engine

  • Compare the PassKit bill with a developer's cost
  • If it pays, build the engine from the Developer Blueprint
  • Move venues across one at a time

Goal: own the technology once revenue justifies it.
