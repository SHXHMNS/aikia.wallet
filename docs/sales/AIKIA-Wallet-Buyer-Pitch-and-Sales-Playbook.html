AIKIA.WALLET | A members club in your customers’ phone wallet

The regulars are already there. Give them a reason to return.

A managed digital loyalty and customer-retention service for independent cafés, bakeries and food venues. Guests join with a QR scan. Your team recognizes regulars. AIKIA helps you keep the program active.

THE PROMISE

Turn more first visits into recognized regulars with a branded wallet card, simple rewards and relevant follow-up.

FOR THE GUEST

A familiar card in Apple Wallet or Google Wallet. No separate café app to find, download or remember.

FOR THE OWNER

A direct, consent-based way to recognize members, encourage another visit and learn which activity brings people back.

THE MODEL

A one-time setup plus a flat monthly service fee. No percentage commission on member sales in the proposed pricing model.

A café membership experience, designed and supported by AIKIA.
01 | THE OPPORTUNITY

The café problem is what happens after the first visit.

Good hospitality creates the first visit. The harder job is giving guests a simple reason to choose you again, and giving the owner a way to learn whether it worked.

PAPER CARDS DISAPPEAR

Guests forget them. Staff cannot connect a stamp to a customer or understand what brings people back.

STANDALONE APPS ASK TOO MUCH

A guest may not want another download for one local café. A wallet pass keeps the join flow lighter.

BROAD DISCOUNTS WASTE MARGIN

A public discount reaches people who might never return and guests who would have paid full price anyway.

QUIET HOURS ARE HARD TO ACT ON

When the café slows down, owners need a relevant way to reach opted-in members and measure the response.

The goal is not more messages. It is more useful reasons to come back, measured against a real baseline.
02 | HOW IT WORKS

A short guest journey. A managed owner experience.

The intended experience keeps joining and staff actions simple, while AIKIA handles the setup and helps the owner use the program consistently.

1. INVITE

A guest scans the venue’s QR code at the counter, on a table tent or on a receipt.

2. JOIN

The guest enters the requested details, sees the consent language and adds the branded pass to their phone wallet.

3. RECOGNIZE

At a visit, staff scan the member card and apply the venue’s chosen stamp, points or eligible reward.

4. RE-ENGAGE

The venue can run a relevant offer or campaign for members who have agreed to receive it.

5. LEARN

The owner reviews sign-ups, visits, redemptions, campaign response and opt-outs with AIKIA.

AIKIA’s service layer is part of the offer: card design, reward setup, staff onboarding, campaign support and reporting.
03 | PRODUCT SCOPE

Start with the repeat-visit loop. Expand after the pilot proves it.

The long-term concept includes more than a digital stamp card. The first venue pilot should stay narrow enough to operate reliably and measure clearly.

PILOT FOUNDATION

One venue; branded Apple/Google Wallet pass; QR join flow; member consent; one simple loyalty mechanic; staff scanning; a basic member and visit report.

CAMPAIGN LAYER

A welcome message and one or two focused campaigns, such as a quiet-hour offer or a lapsed-member invitation. Frequency and eligibility rules are agreed with the owner.

STATUS & PERSONALITY

Ink, Chrome and Pink tiers can make membership feel distinctive. Use them only when the venue has a clear, manageable rule for earning status.

LATER OPTIONS

Nearby nudges (Halo), Fill My Tables, prepaid balances, Gift a Coffee, Coffee Club, multi-outlet reporting, POS links and the Circle network are roadmap concepts. Each needs availability, legal and operating checks before it is sold as live.

The pilot scope must match the capability that is actually configured, tested and supported for that venue.
04 | THE BUSINESS CASE

Talk about contribution, not vanity metrics.

The service is meant to support repeat visits and direct customer relationships. The venue’s own baseline and transaction economics determine whether it pays back.

MEASURE

New opted-in members; member visits; reward cost and redemption; campaign-attributed visits; average ticket; contribution margin; opt-outs; staff effort.

COMPARE

Agree a baseline before launch. Compare like-for-like periods and account for seasonality, holidays, menu changes and other promotions.

CALCULATE

Incremental contribution = attributable incremental visits × contribution per visit. Net contribution after the service fee = incremental contribution − monthly fee − reward and campaign costs.

ILLUSTRATION ONLY

At a proposed Signature fee of INR 9,999/month, an INR 350 average ticket and an assumed 50% contribution margin imply INR 175 contribution per extra visit. About 58 incremental visits would cover the monthly fee before setup, staff time, tax and reward costs.

Illustration, not a forecast or a guarantee. Replace every assumption with the venue’s figures and measured pilot results.

SIGNATURE | INR 9,999/MONTH + INR 25,000 SETUP

06 | THE SALES CONVERSATION

A three-minute demo the owner can feel.

Keep the meeting about the owner’s customer problem. Let the owner use a demo card on their own phone; explain what is sample data and what is currently operational.

0:00-0:30 | ASK

“How do you recognize regulars today? How do you reach them when a weekday is quiet? What do you currently measure?”

0:30-1:15 | JOIN

Invite the owner to scan a demo QR, review the consent and add a sample card. Show how the venue’s brand would appear.

1:15-2:00 | USE

Demonstrate a scan and one reward update using a test member. Explain exactly which parts are live and which are prototype/sample.

2:00-2:40 | ACT

Show one proposed campaign and explain who would receive it, what limits apply and how results would be measured.

2:40-3:00 | NEXT STEP

Agree a pilot objective, baseline, venue contact, reward rule and a date to review results. Send a written scope and commercial terms.

30-second pitch: “We help cafés turn more first visits into regulars. Guests join through a QR code and keep your branded membership card in their phone wallet. We set up the card and rewards, help your team run focused member campaigns, and measure visits and redemptions with you. The aim is to make repeat visits easier to earn and easier to understand.”
07 | OBJECTIONS & ANSWERS

Answer the hard questions plainly.

Trust comes from acknowledging the trade-off, then showing the operating plan. Avoid promising a result before a pilot has measured it.

“WE ALREADY HAVE A STAMP CARD.”

“Keep the reward idea. The wallet version is easier to carry and can connect a visit to a member record, subject to the final setup.”

“MY STAFF ARE TOO BUSY.”

“The intended counter flow is one invitation and one scan. We will train the team and time the flow during the pilot; if it slows service, we simplify it.”

“WILL GUESTS WANT THIS?”

“We keep sign-up short and make marketing consent clear. We measure joins and opt-outs; guests should be able to stop receiving offers.”

“CAN YOU GUARANTEE MORE SALES?”

“No. We can agree a baseline, run a focused pilot and report what changed. Sales depend on your offer, margins, footfall and execution.”

“WHY A MONTHLY FEE?”

“It covers the configured program and the agreed support/campaign work. We will compare the fee with incremental contribution, not just top-line sales.”

“WHO OWNS OUR CUSTOMER DATA?”

“The intended terms say the venue owns its member list and may export it. We will document processors, access, retention, deletion and consent before onboarding.”

If a feature, integration or policy is not verified for the proposed pilot, say so and put it in the written scope as a dependency or future option.
08 | ONBOARDING & MEASUREMENT

A pilot should answer one commercial question.

A short, measurable pilot makes the next decision easier for both sides. The exact duration and price should be agreed in writing.

BEFORE | DEFINE

Choose one business goal (for example, lapsed-member return); record current visits, average ticket and reward cost; agree eligibility, consent copy and campaign limits.

SETUP | PREPARE

Collect logo, venue details, reward rule, opening hours, staff list and approved offer. Configure and test on representative iPhone and Android devices.

LAUNCH | TRAIN

Place the join QR, train staff, run test scans and redemption, and provide a manual fallback for outages or guests without a supported wallet.

REVIEW | DECIDE

Review membership growth, repeat visits, redemptions, attributed contribution, opt-outs and staff friction. Decide to continue, adjust or stop.

Suggested pilot scorecard: baseline period + pilot period, target cohort, campaign dates, attribution method, reward liability and a named venue owner.
09 | TRUST, DATA & OPERATIONS

The venue should know what it is buying.

The product handles member identity, visits and sometimes stored-value concepts. A professional sales conversation explains the rules before asking a venue to entrust its customers to the service.

CONSENT & MESSAGES

Show plain-language consent at sign-up. Separate service messages from marketing where required. Set an agreed frequency cap, honor opt-outs and avoid sending sensitive personal details in notifications.

ACCESS & ISOLATION

Each venue should access only its own members and reports. Staff permissions, owner permissions and AIKIA admin access should be defined.

RECORDS & RECOVERY

Keep an auditable record of reward changes, back up data, test restoration and document how a guest recovers a card or corrects an error.

PAYMENTS & LIABILITY

Define who funds and honors rewards or prepaid balances, what happens on cancellation, and how refunds, taxes and invoices are handled. Prepaid balance features require legal/accounting review before activation.

SUPPORT

Name the support channel, response expectations, outage fallback and escalation contact before launch.

These are product commitments to formalize. Confirm the privacy notice, venue agreement, data processing terms, India legal/tax treatment and provider settings before live onboarding.
10 | CLOSE & FOLLOW-UP

Close with a low-friction next step.

The best close is a specific pilot decision, not pressure. Make the owner’s commitment small and the measurement plan clear.

ASK

“If we could improve one thing about repeat visits in the next 60 days, what should it be?”

CONFIRM

“Who needs to approve the offer, the member consent and the monthly spend?”

PROPOSE

“Let’s map a pilot for one venue, one reward and one campaign. We’ll document the setup, price, measures, responsibilities and exit terms before we start.”

NEXT STEP

Book a 30-minute discovery and baseline session. Request the venue logo, current loyalty rule, average ticket range, quiet periods and current repeat-visit estimate.

FOLLOW-UP MESSAGE

“Thanks for the conversation. Based on what you shared, the pilot would focus on [goal] at [venue]. We’ll measure [baseline + outcome], using [reward/campaign], over [dates]. I’ll send a scope with the verified features, data/consent terms and confirmed pricing for your review.”

Recommended next step: select one friendly café for a tightly scoped pilot and validate the guest flow, operations and measurement before broad selling.
APPENDIX | INTERNAL SALES CONTROLS

Seller checklist: ready before the first buyer meeting.

The existing AIKIA Wallet folder contains detailed product and launch blueprints, but it does not establish that a production service is implemented. Use this checklist before presenting the offer as available.

DEMO

Have a verified demo card, tested join QR, working scanner/update flow and sample dashboard. Label sample data. Test the exact devices and wallet platforms used in the meeting.

SCOPE

Write down what is operational today, what is configured for this venue, what depends on PassKit/provider approval and what remains roadmap.

COMMERCIALS

Approve pricing, setup fee, founding offer, term, support scope, provider charges, taxes, refund/cancellation rules and any price lock before sending a quote.

CLAIMS

Do not claim a measured sales lift, profit, fixed notification reach/radius, guaranteed return, full wallet support or live integrations without venue-specific evidence.

PILOT READINESS

Confirm legal entity/invoicing, venue agreement, privacy/consent, data export/deletion, tested backup/recovery, roles, support contact and outage fallback.

The next concrete milestone is a verified end-to-end pilot, followed by measured case-study evidence. Until then, present this as a proposed service and pilot plan.
