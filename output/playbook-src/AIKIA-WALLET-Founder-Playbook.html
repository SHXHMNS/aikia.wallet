00 / SUMMARY

The short version

aikia.wallet can serve almost any business that has repeat customers, if we treat it as one membership engine with a small set of rules that each business configures, not as a custom app per industry.

What we sell

A venue-branded card in the customer's phone wallet, a staff scanner, an owner dashboard and a ledger that records every visit and reward. The venue chooses what counts (a purchase, a visit, an attended class), what is earned (stamps, points, tiers, packs, badges) and how members are reached (wallet, WhatsApp, SMS).

Why it can scale

The same ledger and wallet pass serve a 400-member cafe and a 40,000-member supermarket. Differences live in presets, rules and integrations. Sales scale through partners (POS vendors, agencies, brands, distributors), because direct selling to millions of small venues does not pay.

The biggest legal line

Points and visits are low-risk. Stored money (wallet balance, top-ups, transfers) is a regulated payment product under RBI rules. Recommendation: launch with points only, add venue-run prepaid packs later with counsel, never hold customer money. Data protection (DPDP Act) applies from day one, and fully from about May 2027.

The money

Build: about Rs 34.9 lakh to Rs 54.5 lakh with a small in-house team over about 8 months (Option B). Run: about Rs 2.5 lakh a month at pilot, Rs 9.4 lakh at growth, Rs 38.5 lakh at scale. Break-even: roughly 386 paying venues at growth-stage cost, if the average venue pays about Rs 3,500 a month.

What we recommend, in order

  1. Pick three pilot venues in Wave 1 types (a cafe, a sweet shop or bakery, a salon or court). RUH can be pilot zero for the sweets vertical.
  2. Decide the money model now (Chart 4): points only. Put venue-run prepaid packs on the Phase 2 list.
  3. Fix the legal base before the first paying customer: company, GST, trademark check on the name, privacy policy and venue data-processing terms, DLT registration for SMS.
  4. Build the integration and messaging layer next. POS and WhatsApp are what turn a pilot into a repeatable product.
  5. Treat education as a separate programme: start with one college campus (the PU PULSE idea) before schools, because minors' data raises the compliance bar.

01 / THE PRODUCT

One engine, many shop types

Every loyalty idea in this document is built from the same six parts. A new business type is a new combination of these parts, not a new product.

1. Trigger

What counts. A paid bill closed in the POS, a staff scan, a booking marked attended, a class check-in, a teacher marking attendance, a scanned invoice.

2. Accrual

What is earned. A stamp, points per rupee, a visit, a session credit, a house point, a bonus multiplier at off-peak hours.

3. Reward

What is unlocked. A free item, rupees off, an add-on service, priority booking, a badge, a brand-funded coupon, a guest pass.

4. Tier

Where the member stands. By spend, by visits or by time in the programme: Ink, Chrome, Pink in the current build.

5. Limits and expiry

Caps per day, one-time redemption, points expiry, pack validity, exclusions, reversal on returns or cancellations.

6. Channel

How the member is reached: the wallet card itself, WhatsApp, SMS, a nearby-store alert, a booking or reorder link.

What the gap looks like

Large chains

Already served by enterprise platforms (custom pricing; Capillary is positioned for enterprise) and by their own apps. They buy on integration depth, security and support. Long sales cycles.

Small and mid-sized venues

Often use paper punch cards, a WhatsApp list or nothing. Mid-market tools exist (Zoho Loyalty is listed at about Rs 2,000 a month on a competitor's comparison page; unverified). The opening is a card that needs no app download, sets up in an hour and is priced like a utility.

Education

Large institution counts, but mostly served by ERP and fee-payment tools. Digital ID, attendance engagement and campus-merchant networks are thinly served. Higher compliance burden, so fewer fast followers.

Our edge

No-download wallet card; owner-friendly set-up; vertical presets; managed program design from the aikia growth agency; a ledger that is audit-ready from day one.

Competition check still needed. We have not compared features and pricing of Indian competitors in detail (for example Zoho Loyalty, Capillary, POS-bundled loyalty tools, Google Wallet pass vendors, QR stamp-card apps). Do a one-week competitor teardown before fixing prices.

04 / CHART 1

Joining (steps 1 to 4)

A QR poster or link starts it. The member gives a phone number and ticks consent once; the platform stores the consent record and issues the card. No app to download.

Earning (steps 5 to 8)

Staff scan the card and enter the bill or mark the visit. The rules engine applies earn rules, tier moves and limits, and blocks duplicates. The result is written to the ledger and the card refreshes.

Redeeming and returning (9 to 12)

When a reward unlocks, staff confirm it once and the cost is logged. Automated, consent-based messages (birthday, lapse, nearby, campaign) bring the member back to step 5.

05 / CHART 2

Who touches what

Members use the join page and the wallet card. Staff use the scanner. Owners use the dashboard. Only the aikia team uses the platform console, and every action there is logged.

What stays swappable

The wallet engine and the messaging provider sit behind adapters, so a provider's price rise or rule change does not force a rebuild.

Where money is

Payments for purchases go through the venue's own gateway. The platform records rewards and visits, not funds.

07 / EDUCATION

Schools and colleges

In education the product is a digital identity and engagement pass, not a spend-and-earn card. Rewards are mostly recognition, access and campus convenience. The compliance bar is higher because many users are children.

Colleges and universities

  • Campus ID pass in the phone wallet: library, hostel, lab, bus, exam hall.
  • Attendance and event check-in by QR; festival and fest tickets.
  • Campus merchant network: cafeteria, stationery, print shops and nearby cafes give student discounts. The college earns from the network; students use one card. This is the PU PULSE idea.
  • Placement and alumni: milestones, alumni membership with renewal and event access.
  • Notices from departments, with opt-in.

Sales: Registrar, student affairs or the innovation cell; pilot with one department or hostel before a campus rollout.

Schools (K-12)

  • Parent-owned account with child sub-profiles. The adult is the contract party and consent-giver.
  • Student pass: ID, library, bus, canteen, event entry.
  • Recognition, not ranking: badges, reading streaks, house points. No public leaderboards of children.
  • School-run canteen or stationery credit as venue-run prepaid, never held by aikia.
  • Parent notices: PTM reminders, holidays, event passes.

Sales: Principal and trustees; longer cycle, annual contract, per-campus pricing.

Wallet card behaviour to know

  • Google Wallet works for Android users now. Developer-authored push messages are reported as not supported by Google's own FAQ; third-party sources describe limited update notifications (about three per pass per 24 hours, near a relevant time). Plan on card updates, geofence-style alerts and WhatsApp for outreach, and verify the current Google rules before promising any message type.
  • Apple Wallet needs the Apple Developer account and a pass certificate. Until then, iPhone users need a fallback: a web card or a PassKit-issued pass.
  • Feature-phone and no-wallet users need a fallback card (a link that shows a QR and balance) and SMS notices.
  • A pass shows data on the lock screen. Keep anything sensitive (treatment, medical, student grades) off it.
09 / HOW TO SCALE

From three pilots to thousands of venues

1. Productise before you grow

Write one preset per vertical, one onboarding checklist per vertical, one reporting template per vertical. A new venue should go live in under an hour with no developer. Every request for "custom" is first tried as a rule or preset.

2. Integrations are the real scaling lever

Chains will not key a bill into a second screen. Wave 1 integrations should cover the POS tools your target venues already use, Shopify for online-first brands, one booking tool for salons and courts, and a CSV or webhook fallback. Confirm each vendor's API access terms before promising.

3. Sell through partners

Direct selling to millions of small venues does not pay. Partners who already visit venues: POS resellers, accounting and GST consultants, payment-device agents, distributors and FMCG field teams (brand-funded kirana programmes), franchise consultants, college innovation cells, and the aikia agency's own clients.

4. Price on value, not on features

Anchor on repeat revenue the venue can see. Keep three plans, publish them, and charge setup only for managed work (program design, creative, training).

10 / LEGAL AND MONEY

The single most important legal decision is which of these three the product does. Everything else in the legal section follows from it.

A. What we need

Clear programme terms, expiry rules, consent, a ledger that can report points issued, redeemed and expired. No RBI authorisation is expected to be needed for points with no cash value and no cash-out, but get that in writing from counsel.

B. What we need

Pack terms (validity, refund, transfer, expiry), the venue collecting money in its own account, consumer-law review, and a design where the balance can be spent only at that venue. Wait for the final RBI direction before launch.

C. What it would take

RBI authorisation as a prepaid-instrument issuer (reported net worth thresholds of Rs 5 crore rising to Rs 15 crore under the 2026 draft) or a partnership with a licensed bank or issuer. Not a start-up-stage project.

B. Money: the RBI line

  • Prepaid payment instruments (PPIs) are regulated by the RBI. The 2021 Master Direction treats a "closed system" PPI (usable only to buy the issuer's own goods and services, no cash-out, not usable for third-party payments) as outside the authorisation requirement. Gift instruments were capped at Rs 10,000 and are non-reloadable.
  • Draft 2026 changes (comments were due 22 May 2026; final status not confirmed in this research). The draft reportedly removes the closed-system category but keeps an exemption for PPIs issued by entities that are not marketplaces. Marketplace-issued PPIs would need a licensed issuer. Non-bank applicants would need a net worth of Rs 5 crore, rising to Rs 15 crore.
  • What this means for us. A venue honouring its own pack at its own counter is the safest shape. A wallet balance usable across venues, on a marketplace, or transferable between people moves into licensed territory. Loyalty points with no cash value and no cash-out are generally treated differently from PPIs, but this research did not find RBI text that says so directly: ask counsel for a written view.
  • Payment aggregation. If aikia collected customers' payments for venues and settled to them, it would be acting as a payment aggregator, which the RBI's 2025 directions regulate (authorisation required, with net-worth thresholds of Rs 15 crore at application rising to Rs 25 crore, per press reports). Avoid: let each venue use its own gateway account.
  • Savings and instalment schemes. Jewellers, saree shops and others run "pay monthly, redeem later" schemes. These can fall under rules on unregulated deposits and chit schemes. Do not offer or administer them through the product without counsel.

C. Tax treatment of rewards

  • GST on vouchers and gift cards is a developing area: tribunals and advance-ruling bodies have held that tax is levied on the underlying supply at redemption rather than on the voucher itself (for example the Kalyan Jewellers appellate ruling cited in commentary). Reward points were once characterised as actionable claims. The venue's CA should decide treatment; aikia should make the ledger able to report issued, redeemed and expired points by period.
  • For dealer and channel incentives, TDS on business benefits (Section 194R) can apply to the payer; confirm limits with a CA before building channel loyalty.

E. Messaging

  • SMS: register the company as a principal entity on a telecom operator's DLT platform (one-time fee reported around Rs 5,900), then register sender headers and templates. Unregistered business SMS is blocked.
  • WhatsApp: use an official Business Solution Provider. Meta India rates reported for 2026: about Rs 0.86 per marketing message and Rs 0.115 per utility message, plus provider markup and GST. Templates need approval; opt-in must be recorded. Check Meta's rate card before pricing; it changes.
  • Promotional hours and do-not-disturb: promotional messages are limited by time of day and by the national preference registry. Confirm the current TRAI rules with the SMS provider.

F. Consumers and advertising

  • Consumer Protection Act 2019 covers unfair trade practices and misleading advertisements. Programme terms must be clear on earn, expiry, exclusions and change of terms; changing rules after points are earned is a classic dispute.
  • The Central Consumer Protection Authority's 2023 guidelines on dark patterns apply to consent screens, false urgency and hidden terms. Avoid "only 2 left" style pressure and pre-ticked marketing boxes.
  • ASCI advertising codes apply to offer claims. A "free" reward must be free of hidden conditions.

G. Cyber security

  • CERT-In directions (28 April 2022): report cyber incidents within 6 hours of becoming aware; keep logs for 180 days within India; synchronise clocks to government time sources; name a point of contact.
  • Run an independent web-application penetration test before the first large customer and yearly. Enterprise and education buyers will ask for the report. ISO 27001 or SOC 2 is a later step.

How the cost differs by business model

Cost is mostly support time and a few hundred utility messages. The business case depends on self-serve onboarding: if setup takes a person-day, a Rs 1,499 plan loses money in its first year.

Appointment and capacity venues (gym, court)

More utility messages (reminders, check-in), more support. Booking-tool integration lowers staff effort and makes the product stick.

Chains and supermarkets

Cost scales with members and messages, not venues. Integration, security review and procurement are the real costs; recover them with setup fees and annual contracts.

Education

Moderate message volume, but compliance and relationship work are high. Price per campus per year; budget extra for consent flows, security evidence and onboarding days.

13 / THINGS YOU MAY HAVE MISSED

The unglamorous things that decide success

Product

  • iPhone and no-wallet users. A large share of customers need a fallback; do not launch with Android-only if the venue's customers skew iPhone.
  • Offline scanning. Shop Wi-Fi and mobile data fail. The scanner needs a safe offline queue.
  • Returns, cancellations, voids. Rewards must reverse.
  • Staff turnover. Staff change monthly in cafes and salons; training must be 15 minutes.
  • Language. Hindi, Gujarati, Marathi and others on the join page and staff app.
  • Family and household. Matters for saree, grocery, pharmacy and school.
  • Venue exit. Owners will ask for their data; build export and deletion.

Business model

  • Reward economics calculator. Many owners over-reward. Show the cost of a reward as a share of sales before they publish it.
  • ROI proof. Renewal depends on a one-page report showing repeat visits up.
  • Points liability. Unredeemed points are a liability the venue's accountant must see; export it monthly.
  • Venue churn and seasonality. Sweets, hotels and sports venues have quiet months; consider seasonal billing.
  • Platform dependency. Google, Apple, Meta or PassKit can change terms. Keep engines and providers swappable.
  • Aggregator terms. Delivery platforms may restrict steering customers off-platform.

Legal and trust

  • Name risk. "Wallet" may invite RBI-type questions; consider "membership card" in all marketing.
  • Expiry fairness. Hidden or sudden expiry creates disputes and bad press.
  • Consent for messaging. A joined member is not automatically opted in for marketing.
  • Dark-pattern rules. Keep consent screens honest.
  • Contractors' IP. Get assignments signed.
  • Insurance. Cyber and professional indemnity.

Growth and operations

  • The POS question is the first question every chain asks.
  • Partner enablement. Resellers need demo accounts, price lists, training and a lead-sharing rule.
  • Billing the venues. Subscriptions, GST invoices, failed-payment handling, dunning.
  • Venue success management. A monthly call in the first 90 days cuts churn.
  • Data in India. Choose an India region for the database and keep logs in India.
  • Accessibility. Large text and high contrast for the staff and card screens.
